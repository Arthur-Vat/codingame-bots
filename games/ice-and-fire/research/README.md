# A Code of Ice and Fire: research

Stage 8 of the [new game playbook](../../../docs/NEW_GAME.md): what is known about playing this game well, and which approaches to prototype at stage 9. The early game has its own note, [early-game.md](early-game.md) (E003), a study of what decides games between v004 and v003 is in [game-study.md](game-study.md) (E005), and a study of towers, HQ threats and cuts in v009's games, with the parameters it proposes for E021 to E025, is in [towers-and-threats.md](towers-and-threats.md). A focused scan, done on 2026-10-19: the contest's post-mortems on the CodinGame forum, then papers on games whose turns hold many commands. The generic method is in [docs/research/multi-action-turns.md](../../../docs/research/multi-action-turns.md); this note keeps what is particular to this game.

The contest ran in May 2019; the multiplayer game that followed changed mines (fewer mine spots, 15 gold flat), which our [RULES.md](../RULES.md) already follows. Ranks below are final contest ranks, as each author gave them.

## What strong bots do

- **Everything is built around one tool: the cheapest chain of trainings.** A shortest-path search where the cost of entering a cell is the gold to train a unit able to take it: 0 on own cells and cells a unit can reach with its move, 10 on a free cell, 20 or 30 on a cell held by a unit or protected by a tower, 30 on a tower. A tower stops protecting once the chain has destroyed it, which the searches handle with care (NinjaDoggy, MSmits, k4ng0u, eulerscheZahl, BlitzProg). The same search finds the winning chain to the enemy HQ, the opponent's winning chain, the opponent's reach and the cuts. Our `bronze` policy already has a version of it (`policy/src/plan.rs`, `cheapest_chain`).
- **Games are won by one chain to the HQ after banking gold.** Top-200 games lasted 17.5 turns on average (PED). The meta favours defending, saving gold, and finishing in one move (reCurse). Several bots stop spending when they or the opponent can bank a winning chain soon (eulerscheZahl's "coward" rule, k4ng0u, Nerchio from turn 8; reCurse doubled the weight of gold and income after some turn; Magus's "time to win").
- **Cuts are the middle game.** A cut is a short chain that disconnects enemy cells from their HQ: their income stops and their units die. Every Legend write-up searches for them. Scores are cells, units and towers removed against the gold spent and the upkeep added (eulerscheZahl, teccles, NinjaDoggy, Neumann). Cuts of any shape were needed in Legend (Nerchio); straight and L-shaped cuts reach Gold (Wei-1, eulerscheZahl).
- **The opponent's reply must be seen.** reCurse called the opponent's response "just as important, perhaps even more so", and his memory of opponent replies was "a massive improvement in strength". teccles compares "make k cuts, then defend their best cut"; NinjaDoggy plays the opponent's cuts and greedy moves inside the evaluation; Magus runs two plies of whole turns. Defence is mostly towers placed on the paths of the opponent's best cuts (Neumann, teccles, k4ng0u), because a tower costs no upkeep (k4ng0u, kovi).
- **Level 2 and 3 units only inside a decisive chain or cut.** Their upkeep cripples income otherwise (reCurse, Neumann, Nerchio). Near the top there are more level 3 units, from chains through towers (PED).
- **Moving units is a coordination problem.** Units block each other; the order of moves matters. Solutions: the Hungarian method on a unit × cell score matrix (eulerscheZahl), brute force over the units nearest the front (Neumann), beam search with towers on vacated cells (NinjaDoggy), or the hill climb itself (reCurse).
- **The opening is expansion.** Beam search of the first turns to maximise cells and nearness to the enemy HQ (MSmits, in the first turn's 1,000 ms; NinjaDoggy until the armies meet), or the gold boss's rule of moving to the cell nearest the enemy HQ (k4ng0u).
- **Mines were judged weak in the contest** (reCurse: "Mines are useless"; teccles, eulerscheZahl; about one mine in five games, PED). The multiplayer made them cheaper afterwards, so their value in today's game is untested.
- **Speed is not the bottleneck.** Heuristic bots ran in about 2 ms (Neumann), three of the top four were in garbage-collected languages, and "optimization was not the absolute end-all be-all" (reCurse). The search's breadth and the evaluation decide. reCurse's fast simulation mattered mostly for testing: about 1,000 self-play games a minute.
- **Tree search over turns was not used at the top.** No write-up in the top 17 searches a tree of whole turns; teccles and eulerscheZahl judged the branching factor too large. In the papers, plain MCTS fails on such turns and only constrained variants catch up (Justesen et al., Hsu and Perez-Liebana).

## Candidate approaches

Given our engine: about 137,000 random turns, generated and played, per second on one thread, a state clone of about 1 KB, and single commands applied with `State::apply` ([README.md](../README.md#engine-speed)); 50 ms a turn, 1,000 ms for the first. Ranks 1 to 4 share the pieces above: the cheapest-chain search, a cut finder with a flood fill from each HQ, and an evaluation of a state.

| Rank | Approach | Expected strength | Cost to build | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Plan search** (reCurse): greedy hill climbing over single actions (each move, level 1 training and tower, and the best few chains found by the chain search), keeping the action that most improves an evaluation, until none does; then the opponent's best plan on the result is stored, and the search restarts with every stored reply played on each evaluated state, keeping the worst score | Highest: it won the contest by a wide margin | Medium to high: the action list, the evaluation and the reply loop, in the game's own crate; the evaluation needs tuning | Actions add rather than multiply, so a turn costs a few thousand evaluations; the stored replies catch the shallow traps that decide games, and stop when a reply is blocked (it is cut at its first invalid command) |
| 2 | **Phases with local searches** (teccles, NinjaDoggy, eulerscheZahl, Neumann): winning chain, the opponent's winning chain, cuts with a check of the opponent's best counter-cut, moves by assignment, towers on the opponent's cut paths, trainings | High: 2nd to 4th, 10th, 12th, 17th | Medium: it grows out of today's `bronze` policy | Deterministic and fast, easy to debug and to improve one phase at a time; its phases do not know of each other, which costs (eulerscheZahl undoes moves afterwards) |
| 3 | **Sampling or annealing over the whole turn** (ValGrowth, cegprakash) with the evaluation of rank 1 | Medium to high: 6th, and 2nd in Gold | Low once rank 1's evaluation and action list exist | A different search over the same pieces; neither write-up models the opponent |
| 4 | **Evolution over the turn's commands** (OEP), or its tree form with a horizon past the turn (FH-EMCTS) | Medium, untested in this game | Medium: crossover must repair sequences, and turns here have no fixed length (Hsu and Perez-Liebana) | Beats MCTS when turns hold 10 or more actions; but it stalls in local optima, and the top players found greedy construction enough |
| 5 | **MCTS** over single actions or whole turns, with pruning, progressive unpruning or a portfolio of scripts | Low to medium at 50 ms | High: `cg-search` would need turns of many commands ([MODEL.md](../MODEL.md#framework-gaps), gap 1) | Unconstrained MCTS played like a greedy player in Hero Academy; no top bot here used it |
| 6 | **A learned evaluation or policy**, from top players' replays (PED, 27th) or from self-play | Unknown | High: replays would come from CodinGame's servers (not tried, and not ours to decide), self-play needs a trainer for this game | A stage 10 idea: a learned evaluation for rank 1 or 3 once their hand-written one stops improving |

Ranks 1 to 3 need no change to `cg-search`: they plan inside one turn and search in the game's crate, which answers framework gap 1 (decided at stage 9, owner, 2026-10-17) unless the owner chooses rank 5.

## Proposal for the bake-off

Two prototypes, built on shared pieces first (the cheapest-chain search with towers, a cut finder, an evaluation of a state), each in its own bot under `bots/`:

1. **`plan`**: rank 1, the plan search with the memory of opponent replies.
2. **`phases`**: rank 2, the phases with local searches, starting from the `bronze` policy.

A third only if the owner wants a cheap variant: **`anneal`**, rank 3, the same actions and evaluation as `plan` with simulated annealing in place of hill climbing.

How they are compared: in `ice-and-fire-arena`, on the source's maps, at CodinGame's limits (50 ms, 1,000 ms for the first turn, no time scale, since the plan search uses its time), 200 seat-swapped pairs per pairing: each prototype against `bronze`, `random` and the other prototypes, with timeouts and invalid answers counted. Each prototype's time per turn is reported too. The strongest becomes `ice-and-fire-v001` through the SPRT against `bronze`, and the owner pastes it on CodinGame.
