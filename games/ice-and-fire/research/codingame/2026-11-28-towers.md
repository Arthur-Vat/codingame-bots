# Towers: ours against zasmu's (2026-11-28)

The owner (2026-11-28): "I keep saying we place very inefficient towers, and
I would like to measure it precisely. I also would like a comparison with
zasmu", then, after the first measures: where zasmu places its towers
against us, and which of ours are inefficient.

## Data and method

All 1,038 games against zasmu on the data branch `claude/codingame-games` on
2026-11-28 (one agent id of zasmu; our releases v014 to v034, test and
ranking games), every ply of each side (`ice-and-fire-review
codingame-judge --players NAME --bank FILE`): 7,567 towers of ours and 14,750
of zasmu's. A scratch analysis mode of the plan bot (not committed) printed,
at the end of each ply of the side to act, every tower of that side, and for
each tower built in the ply:

- **its effect on the enemy's next turn**, the same turn played with the
  tower and without it (that one `BUILD TOWER` left out): our cells the
  enemy can take at its next turn (`eval::reach`, with its gold and income),
  the cells of its best cut (`tactics::cut::cuts`, chains of up to 5
  trainings, its gold), and the gold of its cheapest chain to our HQ
  (`chain::cost_to`);
- **where it stands**: steps from the enemy's land, whether a cell of its
  zone is a joint (a cell whose loss cuts land off the HQ), steps from both
  HQs, own units, cells taken that same turn and mines in or next to its
  zone, whether a unit had just left its cell, and how many of the five cells
  it protects (itself and its active neighbours, `RULES.md`) another tower
  of the side already protects;
- **what happens next**: the side's land and protected cells at each of its
  next five plies.

"Border" cells are the side's cells next to the enemy's land.

The phases tell three different stories:

- **Up to game turn 8** zasmu builds 2.4 times as many towers, placed as ours
  are (on the border, over the cells and units that just advanced), and
  they hold: 93% stand five turns later against 69% of ours, and the border
  cells under them are kept 95% of the time against 75%. Our land loses more
  everywhere then (border cells without a tower: 53% kept against zasmu's
  80%): the early fight is where our towers, and our land, do worst.
- **At game turns 9 to 14** ours have more effect on the enemy's next turn
  and zasmu begins its overlapping walls (22% redundant); its towers keep
  the border better (91% against 84%).
- **From game turn 15** the global figures' differences come from here: zasmu
  builds 6.4 towers a game in turns 15 to 20, more than half with no effect
  on our next turn and overlapping, while ours stand far from the front (47%
  four steps or more) and near our own HQ (28%, then 36% from turn 21); yet
  ours keep their border cells as well as zasmu's or better (91% against
  88%).

## What it says

- By what a tower does to the enemy's next turn and over the next five,
  our towers are not less efficient than zasmu's: few of ours do nothing,
  and a tower adds about the same to the border cells it covers on both
  sides (24 points over border cells without one). But ours keep less land
  in absolute terms (their border cells 84% against 89%, a zone cell lost
  within five turns 27% against 18% away from the HQ), worst at game turns
  1 to 14, as the phase section shows; from turn 15 they keep as much.
- zasmu builds twice as many towers, the extra ones mostly from game turn
  13, overlapping and with no effect on the enemy's next turn: walls built
  ahead of threats, with gold it holds (its median gold when it builds is 57
  against our 49; with 80 gold or more it buys nothing in 47% of its turns,
  `2026-11-28-type-planner.md`). It also builds more towers at game turns 5
  to 8 (0.91 a game against 0.38), all of them with an effect.
- Our one placement of our own: 1.28 towers a game near our HQ, rarely on
  the border later (13%), built against chains to the HQ (95% have an
  effect on the next turn, mostly on that chain). Whether they are worth
  their 15 gold is the open question this study cannot settle: a chain the
  tower deters leaves no trace.
- What a five-turn horizon cannot show is whether zasmu's walls pay later,
  when our gold has grown; the experiments that would test it are towers at
  game turns 5 to 8 and walls when gold piles up.
