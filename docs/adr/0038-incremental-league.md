# 0038. An incremental league at full time, with a leaderboard kept in the repository

- Status: accepted (owner, 2026-10-16)
- Date: 2026-10-16
- Supersedes: [0012](0012-evaluation.md), the league of decision 12; [0013](0013-evaluation-time-limits.md), decision 1 for the league of games in Legend; [0020](0020-full-time-sprt-in-legend.md), the league's time limits in decision 5; [0029](0029-ci-scoped-by-area-and-game.md), the league's trigger in decision 6; [0031](0031-arena-games-archive.md), the league sample's source in decision 2
- Scope: framework

## Context

The league of [ADR 0012](0012-evaluation.md) (decision 12) plays every pair of bots again after each release: with n bots, n(n-1)/2 pairings of 100 pairs, so the cost of one run grows with the square of the number of releases and the cost of all runs with its cube. Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe's run after `uttt-v017` ([run 38898298561](https://github.com/Arthur-Vat/codingame-bots/actions/runs/38898298561)) played 171 pairings, 34,200 games, in 89 minutes; a job's limit of 6 hours would be reached near 38 bots. Most of those games carry little information: a release scores 100% against the random bot, and pairings hundreds of Elo apart barely move a rating. The league also plays at the arena's scaled time limits ([ADR 0020](0020-full-time-sprt-in-legend.md), decision 5), 20 ms a move, where bots do not rank as at CodinGame's limits: E030's settings, the best of six at 20 ms, left about 40 Elo that tuning at full time recovered (E032).

On 2026-10-16 the owner asked for a competition between each new release and the other bots in which matches already played are never played again, and for a leaderboard of releases and baseline bots, updated with each release, with a fitting rating system. The same day the owner chose, among options with their costs: each new release plays its nearest opponents and fixed anchors rather than every bot or an adaptive choice; full time; results in a file in the repository rather than on a branch or in an artifact; every release, the random bot and the greedy bot on the leaderboard; a bootstrap that replays history; the SPRT's games kept out; a `LEADERBOARD.md`; the runner's processor recorded with a canary pairing; the results landing in the release's pull request; 8 opponents of 100 pairs; and the old league replaced.

The rating model needs no change. The arena's league already fits a Bradley-Terry model by maximum likelihood over all games, with one virtual draw per pairing so that a score of 100% keeps a finite rating, and gives 95% intervals. It needs only the results of the pairings, in any order and from any number of runs, and works when not every pair of bots has met, as long as every bot is linked to the anchor by a chain of pairings. Incremental Elo depends on the order of games and drifts; Glicko-2 and TrueSkill model players whose strength changes, which a frozen release does not.

## Decision

1. **A pairing is played once.** Each game keeps the results of its league pairings in a file in its folder, one line per pairing: the two bots, the number of pairs, the wins, draws and losses and the pairs' points, the time limits, the seed, the date, the commit and the runner's processor. A pairing in the file is never played again for the leaderboard.
2. **A new release plays 8 opponents for 100 pairs each:** the previous release, the 5 other bots nearest to it in rating, the greedy bot, and the game's first release as a middle anchor (`uttt-v001`, the plain MCTS, for Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe). When an anchor is also among the nearest, the next nearest takes its place; when fewer bots exist, it plays all of them. Its rating for choosing the nearest is first estimated from its pairing with the previous release.
3. **The league plays at the SPRT's time limits:** CodinGame's exact limits for a game whose bot is in Legend ([ADR 0020](0020-full-time-sprt-in-legend.md), decision 2).
4. **Ratings are Bradley-Terry maximum likelihood over every stored pairing**, as the arena computes them, with the random bot anchored at 0 and 95% intervals. They are recomputed from the whole file each time, so the order of play does not matter.
5. **The leaderboard holds every release, the random bot and the greedy bot**, in `games/<game>/LEADERBOARD.md`: rank, rating and interval, games played, and the experiment and date of each release. It is generated from the results file.
6. **The league runs on the release's pull request, after its SPRT accepts the candidate,** and posts its results as a comment, as the SPRT does. Claude copies them into the results file and the leaderboard in that pull request, so that a release merges with its rating, in one pull request and one approval. The SPRT's own games are not used: a test that stops as soon as it is confident overstates the winner's margin.
7. **Each run also replays one stored pairing as a canary,** and reports a result outside that pairing's interval as a sign that the runners' speed has changed. The canary's games never replace the stored results.
8. **The existing results start from a replay of history:** the releases join one at a time, in release order, each playing as a new release would, at the league's time limits. The old league's results are not reused: they were played at other limits.
9. **The league of [ADR 0012](0012-evaluation.md), which replays every pairing after each release, is retired.** The arena-games archive ([ADR 0031](0031-arena-games-archive.md)) takes its league sample from the league run on the release's pull request, as it takes the SPRT sample.

## Consequences

- A release costs about 1,600 games of league, about 35 minutes at full time, plus the canary, whatever the number of releases. The bootstrap of Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe's 18 releases costs about 10 runner-hours. It runs release after release, since each chooses its opponents from the ratings before it; only a release's own pairings can run in parallel jobs.
- The leaderboard's ratings are measured at the limits CodinGame plays, and kept in the repository's history.
- A release's pull request waits for the league after its SPRT, and Claude copies one more result into it. The SPRT's kept result must not depend on the results file or the leaderboard, so that copying them does not run the SPRT again.
- Bradley-Terry assumes that strength is transitive; a release that beats its parent but loses to older releases shows up only through the anchors and the nearest bots it meets.
- Results measured on one generation of runners are kept when GitHub changes its machines; the canary and the recorded processor make such a change visible, and replacing old results after one would be a new decision.
